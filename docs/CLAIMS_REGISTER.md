## 1. Executive Summary & Verification Standard

This document is the authoritative register of every functional, clinical, legal, and hardware capability claimed in the Rakshak-AI user interfaces, architectural blueprints, and technical specifications.

To eliminate ambiguity and prevent safety hazards:
- **Implemented**: Backed by running production-grade code in the repository and verified by automated end-to-end or unit tests.
- **Simulated**: Realistically visualized and wired through WebSocket or state machines for testing and demonstration, but relying on mock external inputs or synthetic environments.
- **Roadmap**: Architecturally designed and specified, but explicitly dependent on external municipal infrastructure, telecommunication carrier agreements, or regulatory production credentials.

## 3. What We Would Not Trust Yet (Engineering Candor)

Before deploying Rakshak-AI in an active public emergency environment, the following engineering boundaries must be understood:

1. **Consumer Smartphone Accelerometer Saturation ($\pm 16g$)**:
   - High-velocity collisions ($> 60\text{ km/h}$) generate transient impact spikes exceeding $35g\text{ to }60g$. Consumer MEMS chips in modern smartphones physically saturate at $\pm 16g$. While our kinematics filter flags `(Sensor Saturated)`, precise peak deceleration above $16g$ cannot be measured without automotive crash-box hardware.
2. **Cellular Signal Availability in Deep Cut / Remote Highway Areas**:
   - In rural valleys or underground tunnels with zero cellular coverage, mobile packets cannot leave the phone until reconnecting to a tower. The offline queue stores them locally, but Golden Hour emergency dispatch remains bounded by tower physics.
3. **Emergency SMS Delivery Latency on 2G / Congested Networks**:
   - Native device SMS uses standard operator SMSC queues. During network congestion, SMS delivery can take between 10 seconds and 3 minutes. It is a secondary fail-safe, not an instantaneous data pipe.
4. **Physician Discretion Over Sensor Inferences**:
   - Kinematic trauma indicators (e.g. speed drop, deceleration spike) are unvalidated decision-support estimates. They must **never** supersede a certified trauma physician's physical examination, ATLS triage protocol, or diagnostic CT imaging.
